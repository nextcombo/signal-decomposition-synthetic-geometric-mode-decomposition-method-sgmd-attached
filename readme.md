# Signal Decomposition - Synthetic Geometric Mode Decomposition Method (SGMD) [Attached MATLAB Code]

In recent years, time series analysis methods have played a significant role in engineering applications. Time series can be decomposed into a series of simple component sequences, and by judging, reconstructing, and identifying the useful component sequences, one can obtain the most basic information about the system's evolutionary behavior [159]. In practice, the measured signal, as typical time series data, is usually composed of different modalities with complex time-domain waveforms and varying amplitudes and frequencies. At the same time, the measured signal contains a wealth of valuable system state information, necessitating further research. However, to extract all the characteristic information of a specific component from the measured signal, it is necessary to separate that component from the signal. As a reliable signal decomposition method, it must effectively extract existing modalities and separate them from each other. Therefore, how to better achieve the above objective has been a long-standing problem, and researchers have proposed many different signal processing methods [160]. Unfortunately, some popular signal processing methods currently in use have at least one of the following defects when dealing with engineering practical signals:
(1) The method requires subjective definition of parameters and is extremely sensitive to parameter selection. Moreover, parameters cannot be adaptively selected. For example, wavelet transform, EMD, etc.
(2) The method cannot effectively decompose signals containing noise, i.e., lacks noise robustness. For example, EMD, LCD, etc.
(3) If there are complex (non-sinusoidal) waveforms, the method will split them into several inaccurate components. For example, LCD, SSD, etc.
Analyzing methods based on conic geometry are a new type of analysis method for protecting the geometric structure of phase space, mainly used to solve the characteristic value problems of matrices or Hamilton matrices in dynamics and control systems, and have been applied to structural damage signals, singular differential equations, and other systems. However, there is no literature that has applied conic geometry to the analysis of complex mechanical vibration signals. In the conic geometry analysis method, the conic geometry similarity transformation, as the core part, has the characteristics of protecting the system structure and maintaining the essential characteristics of the time series unchanged theoretically, which is very suitable for analyzing mechanical vibration signals.
The Synthetic Geometric Spectrum Analysis (SGSA) is a method of analysis and noise reduction based on synthetic geometry, which has unique advantages in the denoising analysis of nonlinear signals. Consequently, this section continues to explore the feasibility of SGSA in mechanical vibration signal decomposition, with the primary objective of decomposing into different mode components. The Synthetic Geometric Mode Decomposition Method (SGMD) is proposed, utilizing the synthesized geometric similarity transformation to solve for the eigenvalues of the Hamilton matrix and constructing single-component signals using their corresponding eigenvectors. Therefore, each eigenvector can be converted into a reconstructed time series, collectively referred to as the Synthetic Geometric Component (SGC). Additionally, the synthesized geometric similarity transformation employed in SGMD preserves the essential characteristics of the time series without altering them, suppresses mode confusion, and can effectively remove noise during the reconstruction of single-component signals without requiring any subjective parameters to be defined.
In summary, by leveraging the principles of synthetic geometry analysis, the Synthetic Geometric Mode Decomposition Method is proposed, demonstrating excellent decomposition performance and noise robustness. Comparative results from decomposing noisy simulation signals and noisy simulation signals with commonly used signal processing methods indicate that the SGMD method possesses superior decomposition performance. Furthermore, limitations of the proposed method are analyzed, examining its applicability conditions. By observing the decomposition effects of SGMD under different frequency ratios, amplitude ratios, and initial phase differences of the signal components, an interval of SGMD's decomposition performance is constructed.
